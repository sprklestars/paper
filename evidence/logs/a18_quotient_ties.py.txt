==============================================================================
A18.1  the identity fails on an exactly tied descriptor
==============================================================================

untied model, g = swap(0,1):  violation 1.170e-16
tied model,   g = swap(0,1):  violation 7.770e-01
  g is still function-preserving: max|df| 2.665e-15
  and canon(p), canon(g.p) still compute that same function
  max|df| between the two canonical forms: 2.665e-15

So the map is not a quotient without a no-ties hypothesis. Both
canonical forms compute the same function, so the failure is in the
REPRESENTATIVE the solver picks, not in function preservation:
with a tie the arg min is a set and the solver returns one of its
elements. A18.2 asks whether that set is ever larger than a point.

==============================================================================
A18.2  how far trained clients are from a tie
==============================================================================

canon divides each incoming row by its scale carrier, so two
PARALLEL rows become identical: cosine 1 after canon is the tie
condition, and it is the quantity to report, not raw distance.

 client  min dist raw  min dist canon   max cos
------------------------------------------------
      0        1.2766          0.9331    0.5647
      1        1.3088          0.9598    0.5394
      2        1.3079          0.9027    0.5926
      3        1.3071          0.9434    0.5550
      4        1.3481          0.9259    0.5713
      5        1.3460          0.9172    0.5794
      6        1.3189          0.9238    0.5733
      7        1.3186          0.9324    0.5653

over 24 trained clients: max cosine 0.6149, min canon gap 0.8776
exact ties: 0
A tie needs cosine 1. The margin is not marginal, and it is not an
accident: distinct units of a trained network solve distinct parts
of the problem, which is why the descriptor separates them.

==============================================================================
A18.3  can an adversary manufacture a tie inside Gamma?
==============================================================================

Gamma is generated by positive per-unit scaling and permutation.
Scaling multiplies row j by a_j, so it can equalise two rows only if
they are already parallel; permutation reorders rows without
changing the multiset. So no element of Gamma creates a tie that
was not there. Check that, then check the out-of-Gamma route.

 gauge magnitude   max cos after canon
----------------------------------------
            0.00                0.5647
            0.50                0.5647
            1.50                0.5647
            3.00                0.5647

Unchanged, as the argument says: canon undoes the scaling before
the descriptor is formed. The reachable route is a unit SPLIT, which
copies one unit's incoming row onto a dead unit and divides the
outgoing column, preserving the function exactly while creating the
tie. Splitting is not in Gamma, and it is the same escape
sec:negatives already reports for dead units.

  split t   own max|df|   undef x     M4+ x   quot viol
--------------------------------------------------------
      0.5     8.882e-16    0.9997    1.0011   9.537e-17
      2.0     5.551e-16    0.9997    1.0011   4.236e-01
     10.0     1.776e-15    1.0025    1.0091   8.973e-01
    100.0     1.776e-14    1.3171    2.3053   1.143e+00
  10000.0     1.999e-12 2181.4364 12115.7774   1.339e+00

So the tie is exploitable, at the same cost of entry as the
dead-unit case: the adversary needs a unit outside Gamma's reach,
and having one it can damage the aggregate under M4+. What the
paper should therefore claim is a quotient of Gamma under a no-ties
hypothesis on the reference descriptor, with the margin of A18.2
as the evidence that the hypothesis holds on trained models, and
the tie listed with the dead unit as the boundary of Gamma.

==============================================================================
A18 conclusion: 'exact quotient of Gamma' is right, and needs the
hypothesis that the reference descriptor has no exact ties. Trained
clients clear it with a wide margin, no element of Gamma can create
a tie, and the moves that can are already outside Gamma and already
recorded as the boundary. State the hypothesis; do not weaken the
claim.
==============================================================================
