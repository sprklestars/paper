==============================================================================
A10.2  is the damage quadratic in the parameter displacement?
==============================================================================
  The adversary moves the mean by (q - p)/N.  If the global loss is
  locally quadratic in the parameter error, damage - 1 should be
  proportional to |q - p|^2 / N^2.  Testing that directly.

 log-sd       |q-p|    |q-p|^2/N^2    damage-1     ratio
   0.50        9.57          0.159       0.004    0.0260
   0.75       22.14          0.851       0.029    0.0342
   1.00       53.61          4.989       0.188    0.0377
   1.25      141.21         34.620       1.201    0.0347
   1.50      403.36        282.457       7.876    0.0279
   1.75     1227.66       2616.599      53.280    0.0204
   2.00     3912.51      26575.965     371.460    0.0140

  log-log slope 0.940, correlation r = 0.99834
  A slope of 1.0 would be exactly quadratic.  The fit is close and
  very tight, but the slope is BELOW 1 and the ratio column falls
  monotonically, so the growth is slightly sub-quadratic rather than
  exactly quadratic.  The mechanism is ReLU: once the inflated unit
  drives its activation negative on most inputs the unit clips, and
  a clipped unit contributes less than the quadratic term predicts.
  Reported as an empirical law with a stated deviation, not as a
  theorem -- the honest form of this result.

==============================================================================
A10.4  does a larger federation dilute the attack?
==============================================================================
  One adversary in N contributes 1/N of the mean, so the quadratic
  law predicts damage - 1 shrinks like 1/N^2.  If so, scale is a
  partial defence and the paper must say so rather than imply the
  attack is uniformly severe.

    N   damage-1 at sd=1.5        x N^2   own MSE delta
   10              15.8059       1580.6        0.00e+00
   20               5.2184       2087.4        2.78e-17
   40               1.3523       2163.7        4.16e-17
   80               0.3000       1919.8        0.00e+00

  If the 'x N^2' column is roughly flat, dilution follows the law
  and a large federation is genuinely safer against ONE adversary.
  Note this is a defence only against a fixed adversary count: the
  audit delta in the last column stays at zero regardless, so nothing
  here lets the server IDENTIFY the attacker.

==============================================================================
A10.5  the norm threshold this suggests, and what it costs
==============================================================================
  The gauge does its damage by inflating the submitted vector, so a
  norm bound looks like an obvious cheap defence.  The question is
  whether it separates attack from honest variation.

  log-sd   |q|/median honest|    damage   canon |q|/med  canon damage
    0.00                 1.00      1.00            1.00          1.00
    0.50                 1.37      1.01            1.00          1.00
    1.00                 4.40      1.10            1.00          1.00
    1.50                28.34      4.37            1.00          1.00
    2.00               253.58    156.98            1.00          1.00

  A norm bound does fire on the large-magnitude rows, so it is a
  real mitigation and the paper should not pretend otherwise.  But it
  is a threshold on a continuous quantity: the attacker picks a
  magnitude just under it and keeps the damage that remains, and the
  server still cannot tell an inflated honest client from an attack,
  because the audit is blind at every row.  Canonicalisation is the
  stronger answer -- it pins the ratio near 1 and removes the damage
  outright, at no functional cost, because it is itself a gauge move.
