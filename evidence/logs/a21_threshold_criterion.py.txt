       PROOF derives (pi > 0.5, where the interior fixed point dies)
==============================================================================
  u(1-u) peaks at 1/4 at u = 1/2, so the fixed point vanishes at
  pi = 1/2.  All columns use the identical generator and bracket; the
  T = 400 pair is v6's own horizon, the T = 2000 pair shows what a
  longer horizon does to the same measurement.

  T=400  c at pi>0.9 : mean 0.9539 sd 0.0052   (the paper quotes 0.955)
  T=400  c at pi>0.5 : mean 0.9832 sd 0.0037
  T=2000 c at pi>0.9 : mean 0.9829 sd 0.0068
  T=2000 c at pi>0.5 : mean 1.0017 sd 0.0052

  criterion effect at fixed T=400 : +0.0293
  horizon   effect at fixed crit  : +0.0289
  the offset the paper attributes to the dropped O(pi^2 r^2) term is
  1 - c = 0.0461; the criterion alone accounts for 64% of it and the horizon for 63%.
  Neither is a property of the dropped term, so the offset is not
  wholly attributable to it.

  log c vs log N slope: +0.00391 at pi>0.9, +0.00428 at pi>0.5
  Both flat, so 'the N-dependence is exact' survives either way: the
  criterion moves the CONSTANT, not the exponent.

A21.2  how sharp is the transition between the two criteria?
==============================================================================
  If pi jumps from 0.5 to 0.9 over a narrow band of r the criterion
  hardly matters; if the band is wide, c is criterion-dependent and the
  paper must name its criterion.

==============================================================================
A21.3  the OTHER claim: does harm at FIXED adversary capability rise
       with N, as 'larger federations are more vulnerable' suggests?
==============================================================================
  The threshold in r falls like N^-1/2.  That is a statement about the
  BAR, not about the harm at a fixed r.  Sweeping N at fixed r:

     r      N=10      N=20      N=30      N=50      N=80
  0.50     10.00      4.04      3.87      3.78      3.92
  0.35     10.00     20.00      8.82      7.98      8.05
  0.20     10.00     20.00     30.00     50.00     29.16
  0.10     10.00     20.00     30.00     50.00     80.00

  Read as multiples of fair share.  A row that rises with N means a
  larger federation is worse at fixed capability; a row that falls
  means dilution wins.  Both appear, depending on which side of
  theta_N the fixed r sits on, which is the point: the correct claim
  is about the THRESHOLD, and 'more vulnerable' must be said of the
  bar rather than of the damage.

==============================================================================
A21.4  is the offset unit in tab:e0 right?
==============================================================================
  v6 prints 'closed form is high by X% +- Ypp' where X and Y are the
  mean and sd of pred/meas expressed in per cent.
  With c = 0.9539 that is 4.8% +- 0.6, and the
  MEAN is a relative error: the closed form exceeds the measured
  threshold by that fraction of the threshold.
  tab:e0 reports '4.7 +- 0.5 pp', attaching a percentage-point unit to
  both.  The spread of a set of percentages is fairly quoted in pp, but
  the 4.7 is not a difference of two probabilities: it is 4.7% OF the
  threshold, so the entry should read '4.7% (spread 0.5 pp)'.

==============================================================================
A21 conclusion: the exponent -1/2 is derived and holds at both
criteria; c is criterion- and horizon-dependent, so prop:threshold must
state that its c is measured where pi exceeds 0.9 at T = 400 and not at
the fixed point's disappearance, and tab:e0's '4.7 pp' should read
'4.7%'.
