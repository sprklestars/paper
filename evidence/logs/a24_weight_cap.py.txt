A20.5  the repaired statement, as a predicate
==============================================================================
  Claim: with a = 0, for every translation-equivariant scorer, the
  suppression applied to a common-mode-biased client in W1 equals the

A24.1/2  harm, honest cost, and resolution at the silent operating point
==============================================================================
  honest cost is MSE on the attack-free control under the SAME
  mechanism, relative to undefended.  resolution is the Spearman
  correlation between honest clients' weight and their true precision
  1/sigma on that control: a mechanism that bounds the adversary by
  flattening the weights has become FedAvg and cannot rank at all.

       mechanism    xfair      cost  resolution
      undefended    4.59x    1.000x     +0.980
  hard cap c=1.4    1.37x    1.087x     +0.970
  hard cap c=2.0    1.92x    1.010x     +0.979
  hard cap c=3.0    2.82x    0.999x     +0.980
     M2 lam=0.25    1.91x    1.208x     +0.980
      M2 lam=0.5    2.82x    1.086x     +0.980
       M1 c=3e-3    1.30x    1.111x     +0.963

  harness OK: undefended 4.59x, M2 2.82/1.91x, M1 1.30x reproduce E3.

  matched at ~2.8x: cap 0.999x cost vs M2 1.086x
  matched at ~1.9x: cap 1.010x cost vs M2 1.208x
  -> at matched harm the cap is the CHEAPER mechanism, and it keeps
     its resolution.  Both easy objections to it fail.

A24.4  eq:suppcost binds the cap: it is not an exemption
==============================================================================
  A capped rule is still peer-only, so thm:unidentifiable applies and
  A20.2's identity eps_2 = 1 - supp must hold for it.  Measured on
  A20's own three-world construction.

                 rule   max|w(W1)-w(W2)|  worst |1-supp-eps_2|
   inv-sq + cap c=1.4          7.633e-17             2.220e-16
   inv-sq + cap c=2.0          1.249e-16             3.331e-16
   inv-sq + cap c=3.0          1.249e-16             2.220e-16

  the two worlds agree to 1.2e-16 -- not bitwise, because the
  water-filling redistribution is float-asymmetric, but far inside any
  weight that matters -- and the identity holds to 3.3e-16.
  -> the cap buys its bound at the same exchange rate as every other
     peer-only rule.  It is another point on eq:suppcost, not an
     escape from it.

A24.5  the cap's guarantee decays in coalition size; M2's does not
==============================================================================
  A per-client cap of c/N held by K colluders yields at most K*c/N, so
  the coalition's absolute take grows linearly in K and the cap is
  inert once K/N >= 1/c.  M2 reserves (1-lambda) of the mass for
  anchor-derived weight whatever K is.  Calibrate the pairs to the
  same absolute share at K = 1 and follow them out.

       mechanism       K=1       K=3       K=6      K=10      K=15
      undefended     0.153     0.369     0.569     0.719     0.830
  hard cap c=1.4     0.046     0.137     0.276     0.459     0.686
  hard cap c=2.0     0.064     0.193     0.386     0.639     0.828
  hard cap c=3.0     0.094     0.282     0.547     0.719     0.830
     M2 lam=0.25     0.064     0.168     0.292     0.430     0.582
      M2 lam=0.5     0.094     0.235     0.385     0.526     0.665
       M1 c=3e-3     0.043     0.127     0.250     0.399     0.569

  (absolute coalition weight share; K/N alone would give 0.033, 0.100, 0.200, 0.333, 0.500)

  hard cap c=3.0 vs M2 lam=0.5: equal at K=1 (0.094 vs 0.094), 0.830 vs 0.665 at K=15
  hard cap c=2.0 vs M2 lam=0.25: equal at K=1 (0.064 vs 0.064), 0.828 vs 0.582 at K=15
  cap c=3.0 at K=15 is within 0.000 of the undefended share: fully inert.
